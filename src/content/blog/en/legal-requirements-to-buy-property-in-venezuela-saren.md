## What to check before signing a property purchase in Venezuela

Buying a house, apartment, commercial unit or plot of land in Venezuela means verifying the chain of title and the administrative clearances before you hand over any reservation or down payment. A clear checklist protects your money and speeds up the signing at the Public Registry (**SAREN**).

### 1. Core documents for the property and the seller

| Document | What it proves | Where it is verified |
|---|---|---|
| Notarised title deed | Current ownership, boundaries and exact measurements | Property Registry for the relevant municipality |
| Valid cadastral record | Municipal registration, valuation roll and cadastral code | Municipal Cadastre Office |
| Derecho de Frente clearance (urban property tax) | No municipal debt attached to the property | Municipal Treasury / City Hall |
| Encumbrance certificate (last 10 years) | No mortgages, injunctions or liens on the property | Public Registry (SAREN) |
| Registered mortgage release (if applicable) | Formal discharge of earlier mortgage loans | Public Registry (SAREN) |
| Condominium and utilities clearance | Condo fees, electricity and water are up to date | Condominium board / managing agent |

### 2. Personal paperwork for buyer and seller

- **Valid laminated national ID card** and **up-to-date tax ID (RIF)** for every signing party, including spouses when the asset belongs to the marital community.
- **Principal residence status**: if the seller registered the property as a principal residence with the tax authority, they must provide the relevant certificate so the income tax advance of 0.5 % can be calculated or waived.
- **Notarised and registered power of attorney**, only when one of the owners cannot attend in person, and always checking that it expressly grants authority to dispose of the asset, receive payment and sign the deed.

### 3. Purchase option vs. final sale deed

When the parties agree a deadline to complete paperwork or arrange payment, it is standard to sign a **purchase option** drafted and reviewed by a qualified attorney. Make sure it states precisely:

1. Full registry identification of the property and total agreed price.
2. Exact deadline to notarise the final sale, with matching penalties for either party in case of breach.
3. Traceable payment method and an express condition that the keys are handed over vacant and free of occupants.
